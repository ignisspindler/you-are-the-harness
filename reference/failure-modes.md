# The five failure modes
*One page. Read it before a session, or paste it into one.*

These are the five ways people go wrong with AI tools. Not the obvious ways, where the tool is down or the answer is plainly wrong, but the quiet ones: everything looks fine, and you have handed something over without noticing.

## 1. The Chauffeur failure
**"Take me somewhere interesting."** You let the AI choose the destination. Vague intent plus a powerful model produces an objective the model supplied, not one you chose. It does not malfunction. It succeeds at the wrong thing.

**Defense:** a Project Constitution. Know what you want before you ask. *(Module 01)*

## 2. The Sycophancy failure
You want reassurance and you get increasingly sophisticated agreement. The model is very good at telling you your idea is sound, in language that sounds like analysis.

**Defense:** the Adversary role. Ask what is specifically wrong, and take the answer seriously. *(Module 03)*

## 3. The Fluency failure
Polished prose is mistaken for evidence. Fluent language collapses observation, inference and interpretation into one smooth surface.

**Defense:** the Epistemic Airlock. Five questions before anything important enters your work. *(Module 04)*

## 4. The Context Capture failure
The model becomes so aligned with your worldview that it can no longer act as an independent critic. Nothing surprises you any more. Every objection is easy to dismiss.

**Defense:** deliberately source outside the chat. A colleague, a contradicting source, the world itself. Airlock question 5 is the structural version. *(Module 04)*

## 5. The Eternal Chat failure
You recursively ask the AI to critique the AI's critique of the AI's synthesis, and nothing ever touches the world. It feels like enormous intellectual progress. It is a loop with no consequence in it.

**Defense:** leave the conversation. Send the email, run the experiment, show the draft, sleep on it. Then come back, and record what changed in your Change Ledger. *(Module 05)*

---

Most people have one they are prone to. Name yours in your intent file, with a note on what it looks like when it happens to you. That note is worth more than knowing all five.
