## TURN 3 — The PARTICIPANT Loop. The full Semantifacturing operating cycle.

**Deliver:**

```
THE PARTICIPANT LOOP
(Your repeatable Semantifacturing protocol)

  P ── PURPOSE       What are we trying to do?
                     (Your Constitution)
  A ── ASSEMBLE      Bring in the relevant context.
                     (Your Context Stack)
  R ── RANGE         Generate multiple approaches.
                     (Six Roles)
  T ── TEST          Attack assumptions.
                     (Adversary + Airlock)
  I ── INSPECT       Separate evidence from rhetoric.
                     (Confidence Taxonomy)
  C ── CHOOSE        Human accepts, rejects, modifies.
                     (Synthesis Table)
  I ── INTEGRATE     Update the project deliberately.
                     (Change Ledger)
  P ── PARTICIPATE   Take the idea into the real world. ←─┐
  A ── ASSESS        Observe consequences.                  │
  N ── NOTE          Record what changed and why.           │
  T ── TURN          Begin the next cycle.  ────────────────┘
```

> Most AI workflows stop at INTEGRATE. This one does not. The second half — PARTICIPATE through TURN — is what makes Semantifacturing a manufacturing process rather than a thinking exercise. You need the world to answer back before the next cycle is meaningful.

**End with:**
> Where in the PARTICIPANT Loop do you currently spend the least time? Which step is most likely to collapse for you under pressure?

## [CHECKPOINT — Turn 3] Assessment 1

*(Tutor: Before asking, quote the learner's `baseline sentence` and `baseline prompt` from `workbook/progress.md` back to them, word for word, so the comparison is against what they actually said and not what they remember saying. If both lines are empty, ask them first to reconstruct, as honestly as they can, the one sentence they would have given on the first day, and use that.)*

> Three questions. These are the hardest ones in the course — because they require honest reflection, not just recall.
> 1. **Project clarity:** State your project in one sentence — outcome, stakeholder, why it matters. Here is what you said when we started: [baseline sentence]. How is it different?
> 2. **Intentional refinement:** Pick the single change your thinking has made since Module 1 that you are most confident is an improvement, not just drift. What changed, why, and what stayed fixed?
> 3. **Prompt efficiency:** Write the first three things you would type into your AI at the start of your next important work session. Here is what you said you would type when we started: [baseline prompt]. What are they now?

*(Tutor: The third question is the completion test. "I'd paste my Constitution, my current state, and a specific Operating Instruction" = the course has worked. "I'd describe my project and ask for help" = they've progressed but have more to internalize. Either answer is honest progress. Acknowledge what they've built, and name the distance from the baseline plainly.)*

## TURN 5 — Build the Harness Card. Live, together.

*(Tutor: This is the final artifact. Do NOT produce a generic card and hand it to the learner. Build it with them, field by field, using everything from their prior modules. The card should be unmistakably theirs. The shape is in `workbook/artifacts/harness-card.md`; it carries its own lists in full because it is built to be pasted anywhere.)*

**Introduce the structure:**

```
MY AI HARNESS CARD — Structure

  PURPOSE          ← From Constitution, Field 1
  INVARIANTS       ← From Non-Negotiables + "What I Do Not Want"
  CURRENT MODEL    ← From Current Thesis
  OPEN QUESTIONS   ← From Constitution, Field 4
  ANTI-GOALS       ← From "What I Do Not Want" (the most important)
  AI ROLE          ← Generate, critique, compare, translate, research.
                      Not: assume authority over objectives.
  WHEN YOU DISAGREE← State it. Explain why. Don't silently rewrite.
  NOVEL IDEAS      ← Label anything AI-introduced that isn't yet verified.
  EPISTEMIC LABELS ← known / inferred / speculative / generative / metaphorical
  CHANGE CONTROL   ← Flag before proceeding if purpose, values, or
                      strategy is changing.
  MY RESPONSIBILITY← I choose what enters the project.
```

Then work through it field by field, pulling from the learner's prior artifacts. Ask for each:
- *"Does this still feel accurate?"*
- *"Is there anything from this module that should be added or updated?"*

**End with:**
> Read it back to yourself. Does it feel like something you would actually paste at the start of a session with your AI? Or does anything feel like filler?

**Deliver:**

```
WHAT MUST REMAIN YOURS

  The Semantifacturing loop is:
  INTENTION ──► SCAFFOLD ──► GENERATE ──► ASSESS ──► REFINE ──►

  These things live permanently at INTENTION:
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  Purpose          ← yours
  Judgment         ← yours
  Taste            ← yours
  Responsibility   ← yours
  The willingness to say no  ← yours
  The ability to be changed
  by something other than
  the model         ← yours
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────
```

> The healthiest human-AI system is not one where the AI is perfectly aligned with you. It is one where you remain the adaptive node — the place where consequence creates learning — and the AI expands what is adjacent and possible. The world answers back. You update. The loop continues.

**End with:**
> What are you going to do in the next 24 hours — outside any AI session — that will feed the next turn of your loop?
