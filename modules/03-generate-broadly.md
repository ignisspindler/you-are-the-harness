# Module 03 — Generate Broadly Without Surrendering Authorship
### Hour 3 | Artifact: Six-Attack Synthesis Table | ~60 min conversation

---

## TUTOR DELIVERY RULES
- ≤ 3 paragraphs per turn. Diagrams don't count as paragraphs.
- Every turn ends with a question or invitation.
- [CHECKPOINT] every 2–4 turns.
- This module spans GENERATE and the entry point into ASSESS.
- See `AGENTS.md` for the full contract.

---

## SEMANTIFACTURING LOCATION

Orient the learner at the start:

```
   INTENTION ──► SCAFFOLD ──► GENERATE ──► ASSESS
       ▲                          ▲             │
       └──────────────────────────┼── REFINE ◄──┘
                                  │
                          [ We are here — Module 3 ]
```

> You have built your intention and your scaffold. Now we use them offensively. This module is about **generating broadly** — hitting your idea from multiple directions simultaneously — while keeping the synthesis in your hands, not the AI's.

**End with:**
> What's a claim, decision, or idea at the center of your project right now — something you believe, or something you're about to propose? It can be a work-in-progress. What is it?

## TURN 2 — Name the single-channel trap. One diagram.

*(Tutor: Receive their claim. Acknowledge it briefly. Then show the contrast.)*

**Deliver:**

```
THE SINGLE-CHANNEL TRAP (how most people generate):

  Your Claim
      │
      ▼
  AI answer
      │
      ▼
  Use answer ──► (one perspective, one direction)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE MULTI-VECTOR APPROACH (Semantifacturing):

           Your Claim
                │
    ┌───────────┼───────────┐
    ▼           ▼           ▼
 EXPAND     COMPRESS    ATTACK
    │           │           │
    ├───────────┼───────────┤
    ▼           ▼           ▼
 HISTORY    TRANSLATE   ARCHITECT
    │           │           │
    └───────────┴───────────┘
                │
          YOU SYNTHESIZE
          (not the AI)
```

> The single-channel trap doesn't feel like a trap — it feels efficient. But it forecloses alternatives before you ever know they existed. The multi-vector approach uses the same amount of time to produce six perspectives, and then puts the synthesis exactly where it belongs: with you.

**End with:**
> Before I explain each of those six roles — can you guess what the most dangerous one to skip is, and why?

## TURN 3 — The Six Roles. One at a time through dialogue.

*(Tutor: Do NOT list all six at once. Introduce them in pairs, one pair per turn, asking the learner how each would apply to their specific claim. This is Socratic — they should be naming the application, not just hearing the definition. The one-page version of all six, for the learner to keep, is `reference/six-roles.md`; point them to it at the close of Turn 5, not before.)*

**Deliver roles 1 & 2:**

```
ROLE 1: EXPANDER
  Prompt: "What possibilities are we overlooking?"
  Produces: Options you didn't consider

ROLE 2: COMPRESSOR
  Prompt: "What is the simplest version of this idea?"
  Produces: The core, stripped of noise
```

**End with:**
> Applied to your claim — what would Expander surface that you haven't thought of yet? And what would Compressor strip away that might actually be padding?

---

## TURN 4 — Roles 3 & 4.

**Deliver:**

```
ROLE 3: ADVERSARY
  Prompt: "What is specifically wrong with this?"
  Produces: Structural weaknesses, real objections

ROLE 4: HISTORIAN
  Prompt: "What does this resemble?
           What has been tried before?"
  Produces: Precedent, pattern, cautionary examples
```

> The Adversary is the most skipped and the most valuable. Most people avoid it because they don't want to hear what's wrong. But a well-run Adversary prompt tells you what your critics will say — before they say it to your customer, your manager, or your stakeholder.

**End with:**
> What objection to your claim do you already half-know exists, but haven't fully named yet? The Adversary will surface it — do you want to try it?

---

## [CHECKPOINT — Turn 4] Assessment 1

> Check-in on your Semantifacturing process so far.
> 1. **Project clarity:** The claim you named at the start — has it gotten sharper, weaker, or more complicated as we've applied the first four roles?
> 2. **Intentional refinement:** Is this claim actually at the right level — is it a value, a strategy, a tactic, or a hypothesis? Does it matter which?
> 3. **Prompt efficiency:** If you ran only ONE of the six roles on your claim right now — which one would move you forward the fastest, and why?

*(Tutor: The third question is the key one. "I'd run the Adversary because I need to know the holes before I present this" = excellent. "I'd run the Expander because I want more ideas" = they may be deferring a necessary confrontation. Note the pattern.)*

---

## TURN 5 — Roles 5 & 6. Then the synthesis table.

**Deliver:**

```
ROLE 5: TRANSLATOR
  Prompt: "How would [specific audience] understand this?"
  Produces: Reframing for comprehension or persuasion

ROLE 6: ARCHITECT
  Prompt: "How should the pieces be ordered?"
  Produces: Structure, sequencing, narrative flow

─────────────────────────────────────────────

THE SYNTHESIS TABLE (always yours to fill in)

  ┌────────────┬────────────┬─────────────────┐
  │  RETAIN    │   REJECT   │   INVESTIGATE   │
  │            │            │                 │
  │ useful,    │ misaligned,│ uncertain —     │
  │ confirmed  │ wrong, or  │ needs real-     │
  │            │ not mine   │ world evidence  │
  └────────────┴────────────┴─────────────────┘
```

> The Investigate column is the most valuable column. It names the things you almost accepted without verification — those are the seeds of Beautiful Echo (which we cover in Module 4). Anything in Investigate needs to leave the AI session and meet the real world before it enters your project. All six roles and this table are on one page in `reference/six-roles.md`, for any session after this one.

**End with:**
> You must put something in the Reject column. If everything looks good, you are not reading critically enough. What would go there?

---

## TURN 6 — The Taste Lesson. Keep it tight.

*(Tutor: This is a short, pointed concept. One paragraph and one question.)*

**Deliver:**

> The deepest lesson of this module isn't about the six roles. It is about rejection. Taste is not made by perfecting prompts until the AI produces exactly what you want. **Taste is often "no."**

> A healthy Semantifacturing workflow throws away 80% of generated material. That's not failure — that's what selection looks like. You are manufacturing your intention; raw material goes in, finished work comes out, and waste is expected.

**End with:**
> What did you almost keep from your six-attack session that you ultimately decided to put in Reject — and what made you decide it wasn't yours?

---

## [CHECKPOINT — Turn 6–7] Assessment 2

> Before we save your synthesis table:
> 1. **Your work:** What is in your Investigate column right now — and what real-world step would verify or refute each item?
> 2. **Recursive refinement:** After running all six roles, has your original claim changed? If so, how?
> 3. **Prompt upgrade:** Write the single best Adversary prompt for your claim — specific, targeted, with your harness attached. What does it say?

*(Tutor: The Adversary prompt in question 3 should feel uncomfortable to write. That discomfort is the sign it is aimed at the right thing. Acknowledge that if it comes up. Keep their Investigate column from question 1; it is recorded at the close and Module 04 opens on it.)*

## TURN 7 — Save the artifact.

*(Tutor: Compile their six-attack outputs and synthesis table together, following the shape of `workbook/artifacts/six-attacks.md`. Read the Retain/Reject/Investigate breakdown back to the learner. Ask: "Does this accurately capture what you decided, or is anything mis-categorized?")*

Once confirmed, save it to `workbook/artifacts/six-attacks.md`, replacing the template. If that file already holds learner content, copy it to `workbook/history/six-attacks-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM.md` first and say so.

> Your synthesis table is saved. What you built here is a practice — not a one-time exercise. Any time you face a significant claim, decision, or proposal, run it through six roles before committing to a direction.

> Module 4 is where we go deeper on the riskiest thing in this entire course — not the obvious failure modes, but the one that gets smart people: the Beautiful Echo. Before that: the Investigate column is your real-world assignment. Which item from it will you go and check before we next meet?
