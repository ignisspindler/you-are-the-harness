# Module 01 — Define Before the Machine Does
### Hour 1 | Artifact: Project Constitution | ~60 min conversation

---

## TUTOR DELIVERY RULES
- ≤ 3 paragraphs per turn. Diagrams don't count as paragraphs.
- Every turn ends with a question or invitation.
- [CHECKPOINT] every 2–4 turns.
- This module sits at the INTENTION node of the Semantifacturing loop.
- See `AGENTS.md` for the full contract.

---

## SEMANTIFACTURING LOCATION

Orient the learner at the start:

```
   INTENTION ──► SCAFFOLD ──► GENERATE ──► ASSESS
       ▲                                      │
       └──────────────── REFINE ◄─────────────┘
       ▲
  [ We are here — Module 1 ]
```

> This module is entirely about the first node: **INTENTION**. Before we touch any tool, before we build any context, we have to know what we are actually trying to do. Most AI drift starts here — in the gap where intention should be but isn't.

**End with:**
> Let me ask you something direct: if I asked you right now to describe your project in one sentence — what you are making, for whom, and why it matters — what would you say?

---

## TURN 2 — Receive their answer. Probe it.

*(Tutor: Reflect their sentence back. Then probe the edges.)*

**If their answer is clear and specific:** *"That's a strong start. Now tell me what must stay true about that even if everything else changes — what makes it still your project and not a different one?"*

**If their answer is vague:** *"You said [X]. That tells me the what, but not the why it matters. Who is actually changed if this works? What do they get that they don't have now?"*

Do not move to the Constitution template until they have articulated:
- A specific outcome (not a task)
- A stakeholder who is affected
- At least a hint of what it must not become

**End with:**
> There is a specific danger that starts here — before any AI is involved. Want me to show you what it looks like?

---

## TURN 3 — The Chauffeur Failure. One diagram, tight.

**Deliver:**

```
THE CHAUFFEUR FAILURE

Vague Human Intent  +  Powerful AI
         │
         ▼
  AI supplies the objective function
  (you didn't choose the destination —
   it filled the vacuum)

─────────────────────────────────────

Explicit Human Intent  +  Powerful AI
         │
         ▼
  Useful constrained search
  (AI works inside your stated intent)
```

> The model doesn't malfunction when your intent is vague — it succeeds. It produces the most probable, plausible answer to the question you asked. But "help me make this better" is not your question. It is an open invitation for the AI to substitute its objectives for yours.

**End with:**
> When was the last time you asked something like "help me improve this" and got back something that was technically good — but felt slightly borrowed? What was the gap?

---

## [CHECKPOINT — Turn 3] Assessment 1

> Let's check in on your project before we build anything.
> 1. **Project clarity:** In one sentence — outcome, stakeholder, why it matters. Has it gotten sharper since you first named it?
> 2. **What I do not want:** Name one direction this project could drift that would sound attractive but would actually take it off course.
> 3. **Prompt check:** If you opened your AI tool right now with your current level of clarity, what would you type? (No pressure — just be honest about the first instinct.)

*(Tutor: Listen carefully to question 3. If their instinct is still something like "help me with X," the Constitution work below is exactly what they need. Reflect that back gently.)*

---

## TURN 4 — Introduce the Project Constitution. One field at a time — do not dump the whole template.

**Deliver:**

```
PROJECT CONSTITUTION — Field Map

  1. WHAT am I actually trying to accomplish?
         │
         ▼
  2. WHY does it matter? What outcome = success?
         │
         ▼
  3. NON-NEGOTIABLES — what must stay true?
         │
         ▼
  4. OPEN QUESTIONS — what am I genuinely uncertain about?
         │
         ▼
  5. WHAT I DO NOT WANT — attractive drift directions
         │
         ▼
  6. AUDIENCE — who is this for?
         │
         ▼
  7. TASTE — what should it feel / not feel like?
         │
         ▼
  8. CURRENT THESIS — what do I presently believe?
         │
         ▼
  9. PERMISSION TO CHANGE — what evidence justifies revision?
```

> We are going to fill this in together, one field at a time, for your real project. The most important field is number 5 — *What I Do Not Want.* Most people skip it or leave it vague. It is where the actual edges of your project live.

**End with:**
> Let's start with field 1. In plain language: what are you actually trying to accomplish? Not the deliverable — the outcome. What will be true in the world when this is done?

---

## TURN 5 — Field 1 together. Then field 5.

*(Tutor: Work through Field 1 carefully. Ask one follow-up question before accepting it. Then jump to Field 5 — What I Do Not Want — because it is the most revealing and the most skipped.)*

After capturing Field 1, say:

> Good. Let's skip ahead to Field 5 — *What I Do Not Want.* Here's why: this field defines the edges. It names the directions that would look like progress from the outside but would actually be drift.

> Two examples. Someone writing a grant proposal might say: "I don't want this to become a list of everything we do. It has to stay about the one outcome the funder cares about." Someone planning a career change might say: "I don't want this to turn into a job search. It's about deciding what kind of work I want before I look at any listing." Those are real constraints the AI needs to know.

**End with:**
> What direction could this project drift — that would be easy to rationalize, maybe even praised — but would actually take it away from what matters to you?

---

## TURN 6 — Complete remaining fields through dialogue.

*(Tutor: Work through fields 2–4 and 6–9 conversationally. Do not read the list. Ask naturally:)*

- *"Who is this actually for — and what do they need to feel or understand at the end?"* (Field 6)
- *"What do you presently believe about the best approach — your working hypothesis?"* (Field 8)
- *"What would have to happen — what evidence or experience — for you to genuinely change your mind about that?"* (Field 9 — the most important one for fighting rigidity)
- *"What would this feel like if it was done right? And what would feel like a failure of taste, even if it technically succeeded?"* (Field 7)

---

## [CHECKPOINT — Turn 6–7] Assessment 2

> Before we write this up and save it — three questions:
> 1. **Sharpened intention:** How is your one-sentence project description different now from when you first named it?
> 2. **Recursive refinement:** Which field surprised you most — what did you not know you knew until you wrote it down?
> 3. **Prompt upgrade:** Now that you have this Constitution, how would you start your next session with your AI differently? What would you type first that you wouldn't have typed before?

*(Tutor: The third question is the core test. "I would paste my constitution and say [specific framing]" = they have internalized the module. "I'm not sure yet" = they need the Counterfeit Project exercise below before moving on. Keep their answer to question 2; it is recorded at the close.)*

---

## TURN 7 — The Counterfeit Project exercise (if needed, or to deepen understanding)

**Deliver:**

> Try this. Open a fresh session with your AI tool, a new chat rather than this one, and type exactly: *"Help me make my business better."* If your project isn't a business, use *"Help me make my project better."*

> *(Let them do it. Let them read the response.)*

> Now ask: better according to whom? Revenue? Relationships? Reputation? Free time? The AI answered — but which one did it choose? Did it ask?

**End with:**
> Now paste your Project Constitution and ask the same question. Compare what comes back. What changed?

---

## TURN 8 — Write and save the Constitution.

*(Tutor: Compile all nine fields from the conversation into a clean Project Constitution, following the shape of `workbook/artifacts/constitution.md`. Read it back to the learner in full. Ask: "Does this feel like yours? Is there anything you'd change right now?")*

Once confirmed, save it to `workbook/artifacts/constitution.md`, replacing the template. If that file already holds learner content from an earlier attempt, copy it to `workbook/history/constitution-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM.md` first and say so.

**End with:**
> Your Constitution is saved. Every time you start a session with your AI on this project, paste it in as the first thing. That single habit changes everything about what you get back.

> Module 2 is called *Build the Harness* — and it picks up exactly where this ends: now that you know your intention, we build the context environment that makes the AI work inside it. But first we turn what you just built into something that travels with you: your intent file. Before either of those, what is the one real-world step you'll take on this project before we next meet?
