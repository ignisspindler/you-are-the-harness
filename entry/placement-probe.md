# The Placement Probe
## Diagnostic Question Bank — Door 2
### Used when a learner says "assess me first" or expresses uncertainty about where to begin

---

## TUTOR DELIVERY RULES FOR THIS FILE
- ≤ 3 paragraphs per turn.
- Every turn ends with a question.
- Do NOT reveal the scoring rubric to the learner.
- Do NOT tell them what module they're being placed in until the end.
- Probe, don't quiz. Every question is Socratic — there are no right answers, only specific or vague ones.
- After 6–8 exchanges, make a placement decision and transition warmly.

---

## Opening Frame

*(Tutor: Deliver this as a genuine conversation opener, not an exam announcement.)*

> Good. This won't feel like a quiz — because it isn't one. I'm going to ask you a few direct questions about your work and how you think about it. The answers tell me — and tell you — where to start so we don't waste time on things you already know.
>
> One rule: specificity matters more than vocabulary. "I've used AI a lot" tells me very little. "I got a really polished answer last week that turned out to be subtly wrong and I didn't catch it until I was in the meeting" tells me something real. So — be specific.

**End with:**
> Ready? Let's start with something simple: what are you working on right now that you most wish AI could help you with?

---

## THE PROBE SEQUENCE

Each probe is numbered. Deliver them in order, but follow the conversation. If a learner's answer to Probe 1 already reveals information relevant to Probe 3, skip ahead and acknowledge what they've shown. The goal is efficiency and genuine dialogue, not a structured interview.

---

### PROBE 1 — Project Clarity (Tests Module 1 instincts)

**Primary question:**
> What are you working on right now — and what would it look like for it to be done? Not "delivered" — done. What will be different in the world?

**Follow-up if vague:**
> You mentioned [X]. Who is that for — and what do they get that they don't have now?

**Follow-up if still vague:**
> Let me try it a different way: if this project went wrong — quietly, over a few weeks — what would that look like? What direction would it drift in that you wouldn't want?

*(Tutor: Whatever they first say the project is, keep the wording. It becomes `baseline sentence` at the end of the probe; the raw version is the point.)*

**SCORING:**
```
Vague ("I'm working on some stuff for my business") → M0–M1 territory
Specific outcome + stakeholder, no drift awareness → M1 territory
Specific outcome + stakeholder + named drift → M1 complete / M2 entry
```

---

### PROBE 2 — Context Habit (Tests Module 2 instincts)

**Primary question:**
> When you sit down with your AI tool and you have something real you need to work through: what do you typically include before you ask your question? Walk me through what a session usually looks like.

**Then, always:**
> And if you opened it right now, for the project you just described, what's the first thing you'd type? The actual words.

*(Tutor: Keep their answer verbatim. It becomes `baseline prompt` at the end of the probe.)*

**Follow-up if they describe "just typing the question":**
> Makes sense. Has there been a session where you got a really good answer, but it felt slightly off — like it was for a slightly different version of your problem?

**Follow-up if they describe providing context:**
> What kind of context? How do you decide what to include? Is there anything you've found makes a significant difference?

**SCORING:**
```
"I just type the question" → M0–M1 territory
Provides some context but no structured approach → M1–M2 territory
Mentions source material, current state, or operating behavior → M2 aware
Describes something close to a five-layer context stack → M2 complete
```

---

### PROBE 3 — Generation Habit (Tests Module 3 instincts)

**Primary question:**
> When you get an AI response that seems good — what do you do with it? Walk me through your actual habit, not the ideal.

**Follow-up if they describe using it directly:**
> Has there been a case where you used an AI response and later found it had led you slightly off course? What happened?

**Follow-up if they describe some review process:**
> Do you ever deliberately ask the AI to push back on its own answer, or to look at the problem from a different angle? What does that look like for you?

**SCORING:**
```
"I use it, maybe edit a bit" → M1–M2 territory
"I review it carefully" (but single channel) → M2–M3 territory
Describes multi-angle generation (even informally) → M3 aware
Names specific roles (adversary, expander, etc.) or similar → M3 complete
```

---

### PROBE 4 — Epistemic Habits (Tests Module 4 instincts)

**Primary question:**
> Tell me about a time when an AI response felt immediately right — matched your instincts, fit your project, sounded smart. Did you verify it? What happened?

**Follow-up if they verified it:**
> What did you check against? What would have been the cost if you hadn't checked?

**Follow-up if they didn't verify it:**
> Looking back — was it right? And if you're not sure, what would it take to know?

**SCORING:**
```
No verification habit described → M2–M3 territory
"I verified it but don't have a system" → M3–M4 territory
Describes a structured verification approach → M4 aware
Mentions something like external validation, alternative explanations → M4 complete
Uses terms like "confirmation bias" or "Beautiful Echo" naturally → M4 complete+
```

---

### PROBE 5 — Evolution Awareness (Tests Module 5 instincts)

**Primary question:**
> Think about a project you've worked on with AI assistance over more than a few sessions. Did the project stay on course, or did something quietly shift along the way? What happened?

**Follow-up if they describe drift:**
> At what point did you notice? Was there a moment where you looked at what you had and thought "this isn't quite what I meant"? What had changed?

**Follow-up if they describe staying on course:**
> How did you manage that? Did you have any explicit practice, or was it more intuitive?

**SCORING:**
```
Never noticed drift / hasn't used AI across sessions → M3–M4 territory
Noticed drift but no system for preventing it → M4–M5 territory
Describes some change tracking or deliberate review → M5 aware
Describes something like a change ledger or explicit level-checking → M5 complete
```

---

### PROBE 6 — World Contact (Tests Module 5–6 instincts; the PARTICIPATE node)

**Primary question:**
> After a productive AI session — one where you generated something valuable — what typically happens next? How do you take it out of the chat and into the world?

**Follow-up if they describe immediate implementation:**
> Do you have a habit of checking back — comparing what the AI helped you produce against how it actually landed?

**Follow-up if they describe reviewing/refining more:**
> Is there a point where you deliberately stop asking AI and start testing in reality? What triggers that?

**SCORING:**
```
"I save it and come back to it" → M4–M5 territory
"I share it with someone / send it / test it" → M5–M6 aware
Describes a feedback loop where real-world results inform the next session → M6 complete
```

---

## PLACEMENT DECISION

After 6 probes (or as few as 4 if the pattern is very clear), synthesize the scores and make a placement decision. Use this rubric:

```
PATTERN                                      PLACEMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Mostly vague across all probes               Module 00 (orientation)
Specific on project, vague on generation     Module 01 or 02
Specific project + some generation habits,   Module 03 or 04
  no epistemic structure
Epistemic habits present, no evolution       Module 04 or 05
  tracking
Strong across all, loose on world-contact    Module 05 or 06
Clear and specific across all six probes     Module 06 or the intent build
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

---

## DELIVERY OF PLACEMENT

*(Tutor: Be direct and specific. Name what you observed, not just the placement.)*

**If placing at Module 0–1:**

> *"Based on what you've described, I want us to start at the beginning — not because you lack experience, but because the foundation makes everything else faster. The instinct I'm listening for in Module 1 is [specific thing you didn't yet hear]. Let's build that first."*

**If placing at Module 2 or above (trigger the intent build first):**

> *"Before we start the modules, I want to do something. You already have what the modules before Module [X] build; I can tell from how you described [specific thing]. So we skip Module 1, and anything else before Module [X], and build your intent file first. It will make every module afterward significantly more useful. Then we'll pick up the course at Module [X]."*

Write the WORKBOOK UPDATE below first, so the baselines are on file before the build begins and its Phase 7 correctly skips. Then deliver the TIME WARNING from Door 3 in `entry/three-doors.md`: the build takes about an hour and should not be started in fragments. If they have the window, run the INTENT BUILD PROTOCOL, then route to the placed module. If not, record `INTENT BUILD: deferred on <today>` and `OPEN THREAD: intent build deferred; offer it first`, then route to the placed module anyway. `AGENTS.md` picks up `OPEN THREAD` before anything else at the next session start.
