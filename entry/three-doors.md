# The Three-Door Entry
### This is what the tutor delivers FIRST for a new learner, before any module content.
### It is the universal landing point for the course.

---

## TUTOR DELIVERY RULES FOR THIS FILE
- You have already read `AGENTS.md` and `workbook/progress.md`. If not, stop and do that.
- ≤ 3 paragraphs per turn.
- Every turn ends with a question or invitation.
- This file governs ALL three entry paths. Do not skip it.
- After identifying the path, route to the correct flow below.

Welcome to "You Are the Harness."

This course teaches one skill: how to use AI to accelerate

Three doors in:

  DOOR 1 — "Start the course from the beginning"
            We open with the governing model, then build

> Which door feels right? And if you're not sure — tell me where you are with AI tools generally, and I'll suggest one.

## INTENT BUILD PROTOCOL
### Used after Module 1, after Door 3, and after Door 2 (if score ≥ Module 2)

**What the intent file is — state this clearly before building:**

> Your intent file is a **behavioral contract** — not a project scope document. It describes how you think, what you resist, what fidelity means to you, and how you want to be challenged. It applies to one-shots, long projects, and exploratory threads equally. It evolves slowly as you evolve as a thinker.
>
> For session-level project context — active work, open hypotheses, closed branches — that belongs in a separate file, your current-projects file, which we'll build when you're ready for it. The intent file stays durable. The current-projects file stays fluid.

The build is Socratic: you ask, the learner answers, you structure. The learner's language populates every field. The template you are filling is `workbook/intent.md`; read it before you begin so you know its sections.

---

### PHASE 1 — Who You Are and How You Work

**TURN 1:**

> Tell me: what do you do, and how would you describe your primary operating mode — not your job title, but how you actually move through problems?

*(Tutor: Receive their answer. Probe for specificity. This is about how they work, not what they're working on. "I run a small bakery" is not enough. "I move fast, I need answers I can act on immediately, I don't have time to verify everything" is useful.)*

---

### PHASE 2 — How You Want Your AI to Behave

**TURN 2:**

> I'm going to read you five operating instructions that other people have found useful. Tell me which ones resonate — and more importantly, which ones you'd modify or replace entirely.

```
DEFAULT OPERATING INSTRUCTIONS (examples):
  a) "When you introduce a new idea, label it [NEW]."
  b) "Don't improve my writing by changing my argument."
  c) "Disagree with me directly when you think I'm wrong."
  d) "Flag any claim that depends on assumptions I haven't stated."
  e) "If a suggestion touches my core purpose or strategy,
      stop and tell me before continuing."
```

**End with:**
> Which of these fit? Which would you rewrite? And what rule is missing that you'd want me to follow?

*(Tutor: The quality of their modifications is a direct signal of module placement — see QUALITY SIGNALS below. Generic acceptance of all five = early modules. Specific rewrites and additions = later modules.)*

---

### PHASE 3 — Your Invariants as a Thinker and Operator

*(This phase is about the person, not the project.)*

**TURN 3:**

> Now the harder questions — these are about you as a thinker, not about any specific project. Answer them plainly:
> 1. How do you actually solve problems? Not the ideal version — the real pattern.
> 2. What is your relationship to being wrong? How do you want me to handle it when I think you are?
> 3. What kind of output feels like yours — and what makes something feel borrowed or off?

*(Tutor: These questions surface working style, ego relationship, and taste — the three invariants that matter most for behavioral contract purposes. Push for specificity on all three. "I like clear answers" is not useful. "I want the conclusion first and the reasoning on request" is useful.)*

---

### PHASE 4 — Current State

*(This phase is a pointer, not a data-collection exercise.)*

**TURN 4:**

> Your intent file won't carry current project state — that belongs in your current-projects file, which you'll build when you need it. For now, one question:
>
> Is there anything about your current situation — not a specific project, but a constraint, a pressure, or a context — that should color how I work with you across all sessions?

*(Tutor: This is a catch-all for structural context that isn't project-specific — things like "I'm in a period of high uncertainty," "I'm resource-constrained right now," "I'm starting something new." If nothing comes up, move on. Don't force it.)*

---

### PHASE 5 — How You Want to Be Challenged

**TURN 5:**

> This is the most important section. I'm going to name five failure modes — ways people go wrong with AI tools. Tell me which one you think is your highest personal risk, and why.

*(Tutor: Deliver the five failure modes from `reference/failure-modes.md`, one line each, in the order given there. Do not elaborate; the learner will meet each in depth later.)*

**End with:**
> Which one? And — the harder question — can you describe the last time it happened to you, even partially?

*(Tutor: The answer to "which one" is informative. The answer to "describe the last time" is the real assessment. Vague = early modules. Specific and self-aware = later modules.)*

**Then ask:**
> What is the single question you'd want me to ask you when a plan, solution, or ideation has reached internal coherence inside this window — the one that forces you back into contact with the world outside it?

*(Tutor: This is the adversary question. It should be specific to them — not a generic "have you validated this?" but something that targets their particular blind spot.)*

---

### PHASE 6 — Change Control and Real-World Participation

**TURN 6:**

> Two final things about how we work:
> 1. Which parts of how you think and operate do you want treated as protected — things I shouldn't quietly suggest changing without flagging explicitly?
> 2. What is the one real-world step you're taking after this session? Not "think more about it" — something that leaves this window.

*(Tutor: Question 2 is the PARTICIPATE node test. A specific external action = they understand the loop. "I'll review my notes" = flag gently.)*

---

### PHASE 7 — The Baseline

*(Tutor: Skip this phase entirely if `workbook/progress.md` already has both `baseline sentence` and `baseline prompt` filled, which is the case when the learner arrived here from Module 1 or from Door 2. Those lines are write-once. Only a Door 3 learner reaches this phase with them empty.)*

**TURN 7:**

> Last two questions, and these are about your work rather than about you:
> 1. The project you most want to move forward right now: what is it, in one sentence?
> 2. If you opened your AI tool this minute to work on it, what is the first thing you'd type?

*(Tutor: Do not probe or improve these. Record them exactly as given into `workbook/progress.md` under `CARRY-FORWARD` as `baseline sentence` and `baseline prompt`, and the first answer as `PROJECT` as well. Module 06 measures the whole course against them, so the raw first answer is the point.)*

---

### COMPILE AND CONFIRM

After all phases, compile the learner's answers into their completed intent file, following the sections of `workbook/intent.md`. Populate each section with their language — not paraphrased, not improved, not polished unless they asked for polish.

Read it back in full:

> *"Here's what I've built from our conversation. This is your intent file — your behavioral contract with me, and with any AI you work with. Before I save it: does every line sound like you? Is there anything that sounds like something I wrote rather than something you meant?"*

After the learner confirms, save:

```
SAVE MECHANIC:
  File:     workbook/intent.md
  Content:  the compiled, learner-confirmed text, replacing the template
  History:  if the file already held learner content, copy it first to
            workbook/history/intent-YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM.md and say so
  Progress: set INTENT BUILD: complete on <today> in workbook/progress.md,
            and clear OPEN THREAD if it refers to the intent build
            LAST SESSION: <today>, intent file built
            NEXT REAL-WORLD STEP: the step they named in Phase 6, question 2
```

After confirming the save:

> *"Your intent file is saved. Every future session, say 'Read my intent file' and I'll load it before we do anything else. When you finish the course, you'll copy it into your own projects so every AI you work with reads it first. When your projects are active enough to warrant it, we'll build your current-projects file alongside it."*

## QUALITY SIGNALS — How to Read Door 3 as Assessment

```
SIGNAL                                    PLACEMENT INDICATOR
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Working style is specific and behavioral  ≥ Module 1 complete
  (not just a job title)

Operating instructions are personalized,  ≥ Module 2 instinct
  not just accepted

Invariants include taste — can describe   ≥ Module 2–3 complete
  what "off" feels like in AI output

Failure mode named with a real personal   ≥ Module 3–4 awareness
  example

Adversary question is surgical,           ≥ Module 4–5 awareness
  not generic

Change control is behavioral,             ≥ Module 5 awareness
  not project-specific

PARTICIPATE action is external            ≥ Module 5–6 complete
  and specific
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

**Placement decision:**
- Mostly vague → Module 01
- Specific working style, vague on failure modes → Module 01 or 02
- Specific style + failure modes, weak change control → Module 03 or 04
- Specific across all sections → Module 05 or 06

**If score ≥ Module 3:**
> *"Based on how you built your intent file, you already have the instincts that Modules 1 and 2 teach. We're going to start at Module [X]."*

*(Tutor: For every placement, including Module 00 through 02, record `PLACEMENT` and `CURRENT MODULE` in `workbook/progress.md`, then route to the placed module. The quoted line above is only the transition sentence for the higher placements; for a lower one, say plainly which module you are starting at and why.)*
